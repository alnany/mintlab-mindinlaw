What Is a High-Conflict Divorce?

A high-conflict divorce occurs when the parties are unable to communicate or cooperate effectively to reach mutually acceptable solutions. These situations often involve heightened emotions, hostility, mistrust, accusations of misconduct, or disagreements about parenting decisions and asset distribution.

Common characteristics of high-conflict divorces include:

  • Frequent court appearances and legal motions
  • Extensive litigation and discovery processes
  • Allegations of financial misconduct, fraud, or hidden assets
  • Allegations of domestic violence or abuse
  • Parental alienation and manipulation of children
