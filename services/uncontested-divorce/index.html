What Is an Uncontested Divorce?

An uncontested divorce is a divorce where both spouses agree on key issues, including:

✔ Division of assets & debts ✔ Child custody & visitation (if applicable) ✔ Child support & spousal maintenance ✔ Any other financial or property issues

By reaching an agreement before filing, you can avoid a lengthy court battle and finalize your divorce without stepping into a courtroom.

Who Qualifies for an Uncontested Divorce in New York?

You may be eligible for an uncontested divorce if:

✔ Both spouses agree to divorce ✔ There are no disputes over finances, property, or children ✔ Both spouses are willing to sign the necessary paperwork

If you and your spouse disagree on even one issue, your case may require additional negotiations before proceeding as an uncontested divorce. Our attorneys can help you reach an agreement so you can still take advantage of the quick and affordable uncontested process.

Uncontested Divorce with Children: Is It Possible?

Yes! You can get an uncontested divorce even if you have children—as long as you and your spouse agree on:

✔ Legal and physical custody ✔ Visitation schedules ✔ Child support payments ✔ Medical and educational decisions

New York has strict procedural requirements for divorces involving children. Having an experienced attorney ensures that your settlement agreement meets legal standards, avoids delays, and is approved by the court without complications.

How Long Does an Uncontested Divorce Take in NYC?

New York does not have a mandatory waiting period for divorce. If all paperwork is filed correctly, an uncontested divorce can typically be finalized in approximately 6 months —depending on the court’s backlog. There are also options for expedited divorce for some cases.

✔ No court appearances are required in most cases ✔ Minimal paperwork—we handle everything ✔ Final judgment signed by a judge without delays

If there are minor disputes, we can help negotiate and finalize a fair settlement before filing. However, if you and your spouse cannot come to an agreement, your divorce may become contested, requiring mediation, negotiation, or court intervention.
